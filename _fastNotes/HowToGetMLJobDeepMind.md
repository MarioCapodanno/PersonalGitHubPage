### Disclaimer from the author

> Note from Aleksa from the future (November 2025): joining a prestigious company is less important than joining a great, tightly knit, ambitious team... If I were you, on the job market right now, I’d look at the smaller companies, the next DeepMinds of the world.

Timeline: one year of intense SWE study, followed by 1.5 years of slower-paced ML learning, and then another 1.5 years of **warp-speed** ML study.

Non-CS background, interested in Android development during a *student* internship. Realized that he was falling behind a lot compared to his successful peers who landed prestigious internships/roles.

Enrolled in a master's program just to exploit student *perks* and attend useful courses like Data Structures and Algorithms, even if not enrolled. Worked like crazy to catch up, failed interviews with Facebook and Microsoft.

Attended a Microsoft ML summer camp, nailed the Microsoft interview but still got rejected due to the number of open positions. After summer school, contacted by the same team that organized the event.

Joined MS as SWE, but since the summer school, his interest in ML stuff was deep and DeepMind was the dream.

At work, learned about SWE and C++ (i.e., [Effective Modern C++](https://www.amazon.com/Effective-Modern-Specific-Ways-Improve/dp/1491903996)). In his free time, learned ML.

Did all of Andrew Ng's Coursera courses, participated in ML hackathons, got involved in ML camp organization, and started reading research papers (felt very dumb reading them). Listened to Lex Fridman's podcasts. Published his first ML article on his blog after 1-2 years or so. But first, placed strong importance on learning effectively (i.e., Learning How to Learn from Coursera).

Switched to a more ML-oriented role internally and attended ICCV 2019 (he was working on the HoloLens). Had the motivation and push to start putting everything he was doing, reading, and building on the Internet.


## ML curriculum

Outcome: cover various subfields of AI, dedicating 3 months to each one, divided into *input mode* (ingest information) and *output mode* (stop input and teach others through YT, GitHub projects, and a blog).

2 hours of learning in the morning and 2 hours before sleep.

Very short summary:

- Neural style transfer (NST): research papers, [projects](https://github.com/gordicaleksa/pytorch-neural-style-transfer), and made a [YT playlist](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S78LQebx6jo&list=PLBoQnSflObcmbfshq9oNs41vODgXG-608&ab_channel=TheAIEpiphany).
- DeepDream: blogs, DeepDream subreddits, and read codebases. Produced a [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6rVrh5gnpwk&ab_channel=TheAIEpiphany) and a [project](https://github.com/gordicaleksa/pytorch-deepdream).
- GANs: papers and implemented [vanilla GAN](https://github.com/gordicaleksa/pytorch-GANs), cGAN and DCGAN models.
- NLP & Transformers: wrote a blog at the end of the 3 months summarizing everything learned, covered papers on YT, and implemented the original transformer from scratch.
- Graph/Geometric ML: started to chat with a DeepMind researcher in this field with the same nationality. Made a video for each paper read and a PyTorch implementation of what he could do.
- RL: different from the others, latest papers. Difficult, so needed [this book](https://mml-book.github.io/).

Other than this, read:

- [Python Data Science Handbook](https://github.com/jakevdp/PythonDataScienceHandbook), chapter 5 — helped me get a visceral feeling for SVMs, PCA, linear regression, etc.
- [Automate the Boring Stuff with Python](https://automatetheboringstuff.com/), skipped first 11 chapters — I felt like going through at least one book on Python since I learned the language on the fly. It also shaped my mindset which improved my productivity in general (in a nutshell: “automate the repetitive stuff”).
- [Deep Learning](https://www.deeplearningbook.org/) — I only read the first part as I realized I don’t really need all of that theory. It should be treated as a reference manual.
- [The Book of Why](https://www.amazon.com/Book-Why-Science-Cause-Effect/dp/046509760X) — I read it because I was genuinely interested in causality and Judea Pearl’s work! Here is my [LinkedIn summary](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/aleksagordic_pearl-causality-intelligence-activity-6807985607432785920-Zxn2).


All of this to build the knowledge to have a *chance/opportunity* to land the job.

## Interview

Got a referral, networking through a conference, and (I assume) with people with the same nationality as yours.

This section is best to read directly from the blog article, but useful links:
- [Cracking the Coding Interview](https://www.amazon.com/Cracking-Coding-Interview-Programming-Questions/dp/0984782850) (the behavioural part). Tips:
  - First, try to solve a problem yourself. Only if you’re stuck, take a look at the first hint, and so on.
  - Always write down the memory and time complexity of your solution before looking at the solution.
- [Introduction to Algorithms](https://www.amazon.com/Introduction-Algorithms-3rd-MIT-Press/dp/0262033844) for O-big **formal** theory.
- [Mathematics for Machine Learning](https://mml-book.github.io/)
- [Getting Started with RL](https://gordicaleksa.medium.com/how-to-get-started-with-reinforcement-learning-rl-4922fafeaf8c)
- [Statistics Exercises](https://www.dpmms.cam.ac.uk/study/IB/Statistics/)
