# How to Read Papers

## Sources

- [How to Read a Paper](http://ccr.sigcomm.org/online/files/p83-keshavA.pdf) (primarily aimed at paper reviewers)
- [How to Read a Paper](https://dslsrv1.rnet.missouri.edu/resources/HowToReadAPaper.pdf) (digest of Keshav's report)

## Idea

Three-pass approach: 1. General idea -> 2. Paper content -> 3. Details

## First Pass: Quick Scan

Take 10-15 minutes, divided into the following activities:

- Read the title, abstract, and introduction.
- Read only the section and subsection headings.
- Read the conclusions.
- Look at all figures, tables, and diagrams.
- Scan the references and mentally note the ones you already know.
- Aim to answer:
  - What type of paper is this? (**Category**)
  - Which other paper is it related to? (**Context**)
  - Are the assumptions valid? (**Correctness**)
  - What is the main contribution? (**Contributions**)
  - Is it well written? (**Clarity**)

Now you can decide whether the paper is of interest to you and whether you know enough about the area to understand it.

## Second Pass: Key Ideas, Methodology, Architecture, and Algorithms

This pass is useful for making comments in the margins. Take 30 minutes to 1 hour, divided into the following activities:

- Re-read the introduction and conclusion in depth.
- Read the design section.
- Examine the results and discussion sections.
- Look at cited works to understand the paper's position in the research landscape.

Now you can summarize what you understood and list all the questions you have that need to be addressed in the third pass.

## Third Pass: Strengths, Weaknesses, and Future Exploration

Scrutinizing the paper can take 1 hour or more, depending on the paper:

- Re-read the paper from scratch, paying attention to technical details, assumptions, and setup.
- Verify the system architecture and its components. Is there any alternative? What are the pros and cons?
- Re-examine the data in the figures and tables.
- Evaluate the paper's assumptions and limitations, including flaws and areas for improvement.
- Investigate the questions from the second pass.

Now summarize your final understanding, insights, and critiques of the paper.

## Additions from Me

- The methodology could vary based on why I am reading a paper and what knowledge I expect to gain from it. The first pass must always be done: the commitment to reading the whole paper in one sitting is usually truncated by other things during the day, and the time requirement is too long, so it will surely be fragmented into multiple blocks. It is better to tackle it with a more structured approach, where each pass has its own scope, and commit to taking notes and annotating questions.
- Produce an output at the end of each pass and be active. Highlight only key ideas and assumptions, make notes even if they are small. Force yourself to reformulate the ideas in your own terms and compare them.
- Use AI just to build a knowledge base of all the papers you have collected and receive suggestions for the next one to read from the web or cited works.
